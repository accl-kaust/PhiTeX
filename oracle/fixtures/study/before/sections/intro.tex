\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Heat maps of physical quantities are often sparse: most of the domain is cold,
and the interesting structure sits in a few hot spots. Sampling such a map
uniformly wastes most probes on empty regions~\cite{candes2006}. This paper
proposes a greedy adaptive rule (Section~\ref{sec:method}) and evaluates it in
Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related}
Compressed sensing~\cite{candes2006,donoho2006} recovers sparse signals from
few linear measurements. Active learning~\cite{settles2009} chooses samples
where a model is least certain. Our rule borrows from both: it is greedy like
active learning, but its guarantee follows the compressed sensing argument of
Section~\ref{sec:bound}.

\subsection{Contributions}
We make three contributions: a greedy sampling rule, an error bound for it
(Theorem~\ref{thm:main}), and an empirical comparison
(Table~\ref{tab:results} and Figure~\ref{fig:error}).
